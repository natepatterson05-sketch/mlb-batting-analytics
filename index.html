THE SHAPE OF THE SWING

155 seasons of major-league batting

Baseball changed. The box score proves it.

From dead-ball triples to record home-run barrages, 128,598 player-season stints reveal how the sport traded contact and speed for power. The long view is not a straight line—but today’s game is unmistakably built around a different kind of swing.

Finding 01

The home run became baseball’s default weapon

Major-league hitters averaged 0.41 home runs per 100 at-bats in the 1900s. By the 2010s, that figure was 3.13—more than seven times as high. The rise arrived in waves, with a major postwar step and another surge after 1990.

Finding 02

Strikeouts accelerated faster than walks

Strikeouts rose from 15.74 per 100 at-bats in the 1980s to 22.93 in the 2010s, a 46% increase. Walks edged down from 9.46 to 9.10 over the same comparison. Modern power has come with substantially more empty swings.

Finding 03

Batting average lost ground in the power era

Decade-level batting average reached .280 in both the 1920s and 1930s. It was .250 in the 2010s. That 30-point gap captures a broad change in how often at-bats ended in hits, even as home-run frequency climbed.

Finding 04

2019 was the unmatched home-run season

Hitters launched 6,776 home runs in 2019, the largest season total in the data and 671 more than second-place 2017. Its rate—4.07 per 100 at-bats—also shows the result was not simply more playing opportunities.

Finding 05

Barry Bonds owns the career power summit

Bonds’ 762 career home runs lead Hank Aaron by seven and Babe Ruth by 48. Four players in the database cleared 700, a reminder of how rare sustained elite power remains even in a home-run-heavy game.

Finding 06

Ty Cobb set the qualified contact standard

Among players with at least 3,000 at-bats, Cobb leads with a .366 career average (4,189 hits in 11,436 at-bats before rounding). Rogers Hornsby follows at .359. The leaderboard is dominated by hitters from earlier eras.

Finding 07

Rickey Henderson turned stealing into separation

Henderson stole 1,406 bases—468 more than Lou Brock’s 938. The gap between first and second is larger than the entire career total of many celebrated speedsters.

Finding 08

Two clubs share the team home-run record

The 2023 Atlanta Braves and 2019 Minnesota Twins each hit 307 home runs. The 2019 Yankees finished one behind, placing three recent clubs at the top of a record book spanning baseball’s full major-league history.

Data & methods

What is—and is not—in these numbers

Data come from the SABR Lahman Baseball Database, 1871–2025 release. One row is one player’s batting line for one team stint in one season; a traded player can therefore appear more than once in a year. No batting rows are dropped. Player names come from People.csv and historical team names from Teams.csv.

Totals are sums of the named counting statistic. Career figures sum all of a player’s stints. Batting average is hits divided by at-bats; the career-average chart requires at least 3,000 career at-bats. Rates “per 100 at-bats” divide the decade or season total by total at-bats and multiply by 100. Decade rates are weighted by opportunities, not averages of yearly rates. Missing counting values are treated as zero, consistent with unavailable/not-recorded fields in early seasons. The scripts in this repository rebuild both the browser data and every report chart.
